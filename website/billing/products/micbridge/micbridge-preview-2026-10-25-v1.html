Commercial-use preview

Personal, non-commercial use is free. The proposed commercial subscription covers internal use of MicBridge by the purchasing company. It is a separate licence for this app, priced by the company’s total employees across all departments: 1–10 CHF 2.90/month, 11–200 CHF 49.90/month, over200 CHF 299.90/month, including applicable taxes. A self-employed purchaser uses the 1–10 band. Each band covers the same app and all internal users; this is not per-device or per-user pricing. It grants no source-code, resale or sublicensing right.

When commercial billing opens, the selected subscription renews monthly at the displayed price until cancelled. Cancel renewal at any time through the provided billing-management route or billing support; commercial permission continues through the paid period. A confirmed provider payment and receipt/invoice identify the company, app, band and paid period. The payment confirmation is licence evidence; merely opening or returning from checkout is not payment proof.

The model is an honour system. Keep your receipt. There are no activation keys, licence-validation accounts or device limits; commercial and personal users use the same ordinary app. Once an approved public download is available, it will remain the ordinary re-download route. Closing the payment tab must not lose the confirmed payment or access to that route. Billing/order records and private support provide recovery; this preview does not invent an individual or expiring download URL.

Applicable billing support, cancellation and refund rights follow the displayed offer and common billing terms. Those terms do not waive mandatory rights. No payment, presale or app-release authorization is created by this preview.

Native app privacy and local networking

MicBridge streams microphone audio over your local network to the receiver you select. The current native source implements no audio-recording feature, app account, payment processing, commercial-licence validation or cloud HTTP service. Other applications, drivers or operating-system logs are outside that statement.

Local discovery and control messages expose computer names and temporary stream or process identifiers to participating local-network devices; the app keeps discovered peer names and IP addresses in process memory. The selected receiver receives microphone audio. This LAN traffic is unencrypted and unauthenticated. Pairing approval is not a security boundary or a promise of encrypted audio.

The app remembers paired computer names and small onboarding/tray preferences on the device. “Forget all” clears the remembered pair list, not every preference or operating-system log. The current source does not provide a promise that every trace of a connection is erased. Opening the external audio-driver website is an explicit onboarding action; that site has its own privacy practices.
